## Three times that bound a kernel

A kernel on any accelerator spends time in two places: doing arithmetic and moving bytes. The Scaling Book models both as a count divided by a rate <Cite id="SB1" at="#where-does-the-time-go" />.

$$
T_\text{math} = \frac{\text{FLOPs}}{C}, \qquad T_\text{comms} = \frac{\text{bytes moved}}{W}
\tag{1.1}
$$

<Gloss>Time on arithmetic is work over peak FLOP/s, C. Time on data movement is bytes over the bandwidth W of whichever link carries them: HBM, ICI, PCIe or DCN.</Gloss>

If the two overlap perfectly, the kernel takes $\max(T_\text{math}, T_\text{comms})$. If they do not overlap at all, it takes the sum. The two bounds differ by at most 2×, and the rest of this course reasons from the max.

<Note>
On TPUs the compiler schedules HBM-to-VMEM copies ahead of use, so overlap is the normal case rather than a kernel-author achievement <Cite id="SB2" at="#what-is-a-tpu" />. Chapter 2 explains why.
</Note>

**Arithmetic intensity** is the ratio of the two counts, FLOPs per byte. A chip has its own intensity, the ratio of its two rates. A kernel is compute-bound when its intensity exceeds the chip's:

$$
\frac{\text{FLOPs}}{\text{bytes}} > \frac{C}{W_\text{hbm}}
\tag{1.2}
$$

<Gloss>A kernel keeps the matrix units busy only if it does more arithmetic per byte than the chip can do per byte of bandwidth.</Gloss>

The right-hand side is the chip's **ridge point**. For the chips in this course, with HBM as the link:

<ChipTable chips="v5e v5p v6e 7x 8t 8i H100 B200" cols="bf16 fp8 hbm_bw ai" />

## A matmul's intensity is its batch

Take $X[B, D] \cdot W[D, F]$ in bf16. It does $2BDF$ FLOPs and moves $2BD + 2DF + 2BF$ bytes:

$$
\text{AI} = \frac{2BDF}{2BD + 2DF + 2BF} \approx B \quad \text{when } B \ll D, F
\tag{1.3}
$$

<Gloss>When the token batch is small next to the weight dimensions, weight bytes dominate, and every weight byte is used B times. Intensity equals the number of tokens.</Gloss>

So a bf16 matmul on Ironwood is compute-bound once the per-replica token batch exceeds about 313. On v5e the figure is 241 and on an H100 it is 295 <Cite id="SB1" at="#matrix-multiplication" />. The batch counts tokens, not sequences. Model sharding does not change it, because sharding divides FLOPs and bandwidth by the same factor.

<GpuAnalogy>
<p>This is the same calculation as for an H100 at batch 64 in decode: intensity 63, ridge 295, so HBM-bound by about 4.7×. On Ironwood the ridge is 313, so the same step is HBM-bound by 4.9×. The decode intuition you already have carries over almost unchanged.</p>
</GpuAnalogy>

<AnalogyBreaks>
<p>On an H100, the 50 MB L2 is a hardware cache, so whether a reused tile stays on chip is partly up to the hardware. On a TPU, nothing reaches the matrix units without an explicit copy into VMEM, and the compiler decides what lives there. Ironwood has 128 MiB of VMEM per chip <Cite id="J-hw" />. A matmul whose weights are already resident has a different ridge, set by VMEM bandwidth. Chapter 2 covers that case.</p>
</AnalogyBreaks>

<Worked title="LLaMA-3-70B MLP up-projection at batch 64 on Ironwood">

The up-projection has $D = 8{,}192$ and $F = 28{,}672$ <Cite id="SB6" at="#what-does-llama-3-look-like" />. At $B = 64$ in bf16 on one Ironwood chip (2,307 TFLOP/s, 7,380 GB/s <Cite id="D-7x" />):

- FLOPs: $2 \cdot 64 \cdot 8192 \cdot 28672 = 3.01 \times 10^{10}$, so $T_\text{math} = 13.0\ \mu\text{s}$.
- Bytes: $4.75 \times 10^{8}$, almost all of it the 470 MB weight matrix, so $T_\text{hbm} = 64.3\ \mu\text{s}$.
- Intensity: 63.4 FLOP/B against a ridge of 313. HBM-bound by 4.9×.

Solving Eq. 1.3 exactly for this shape puts the compute-bound batch at 329 tokens, slightly above the ridge, because activation bytes are not zero. Computed by `scripts/ch01_roofline.py`.

</Worked>

## Lower-precision weights move the ridge, lower-precision math moves it back

Store the weights in int8 or fp8 and keep the math in bf16. FLOPs stay at $2BDF$, but weight bytes halve to $DF$, so intensity becomes about $2B$:

$$
B_\text{crit} = \beta \cdot \frac{C}{W_\text{hbm}}, \qquad \beta = \frac{\text{bytes per weight}}{\text{bytes per activation}}
\tag{1.4}
$$

<Gloss>Halving the bytes per weight halves the batch needed to keep the matrix units busy, as long as the arithmetic rate stays the same.</Gloss>

On Ironwood that takes the compute-bound batch from 329 to 164 for the example shape. Moving the math to fp8 as well doubles $C$ to 4,614 TFLOP/s <Cite id="D-7x" />, so the ridge doubles to 625 and the compute-bound batch returns to 329. The step still gets faster: at batch 64 it drops from 64 µs to 32 µs, because the bytes halved.

The provisional TPU 8i figures push this further. Its fp8 ridge is about 1,024 FLOP/B <Provisional />, so with fp8 weights and fp8 math, decode on 8i needs more than 550 tokens per chip to be compute-bound for this shape. Google's design answer is bandwidth and on-chip SRAM, not FLOPs: 8,601 GB/s of HBM and 384 MB of SRAM <Cite id="G-8dd" />.
