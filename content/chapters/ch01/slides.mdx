<Slide title="Two times bound every kernel">

$$
T_\text{math} = \frac{\text{FLOPs}}{C}, \qquad T_\text{comms} = \frac{\text{bytes}}{W}
$$

<Gloss>Arithmetic over peak FLOP/s; bytes over the bandwidth of whichever link carries them. With overlap, the kernel takes the max. Without, the sum: at most 2× worse.</Gloss>

- Compute-bound when FLOPs per byte exceeds the chip's $C / W_\text{hbm}$, its ridge point.
- On TPUs the compiler schedules HBM-to-VMEM copies ahead of use, so overlap is the default <Cite id="SB2" at="#what-is-a-tpu" />.

<Slide title="Precision moves the ridge">

- int8 or fp8 weights, bf16 math: weight bytes halve, so $B_\text{crit}$ halves. Ironwood example: 329 to 164.
- fp8 math as well: peak doubles to 4,614 TFLOP/s, ridge doubles to 625, $B_\text{crit}$ returns to 329. The step still halves, 64 µs to 32 µs.
- TPU 8i, fp8 everything: ridge about 1,024 <Provisional />. Google spent the die on 8,601 GB/s of HBM and 384 MB of SRAM instead <Cite id="G-8dd" />.
