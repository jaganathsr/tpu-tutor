<Slide title="One thread of control">

- No warps and no hardware switching. The scalar unit issues VLIW bundles for every unit.
- Latency is hidden by the schedule: the DMA for the next tile is already in flight.
- XLA builds that schedule for the whole program, so every shape must be known at compile time.
- The cost: a new shape is a new compile, which can take minutes for a large model.

</Slide>

<Slide title="The MXU: weight-stationary systolic array">

$$
U = \frac{M \cdot K \cdot N}{S^2 \cdot (M + K + N - 2)}
$$

<Gloss>Utilization of one weight tile: M rows streamed, K × N of an S × S array filled.</Gloss>

| 256×256, full tile | M = 8 | M = 256 | M = 2,048 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Utilization | 1.5% | 33% | 80% |

- M is the token batch: decode leaves the MXU filling and draining.
- K = 64 (a head dimension) caps it at 25%.

</Slide>

- Filled by explicit DMA on a compile-time schedule.

<Slide title="Layout is (8,128)">

- Vector registers are 8 sublanes × 128 lanes, so arrays are tiled 8×128.
- bf16 is `T(8,128)(2,1)` and int8/fp8 are `T(8,128)(4,1)`: 2 or 4 values per 32-bit sublane.
- Last two dimensions should be multiples of 8 and 128. Pallas blocks must be.
- Relayouts show up in profiles as copy or transpose fusions.

</Slide>

<Slide title="Devices per chip">

| Chip | TensorCores | JAX devices |
|---|---|---|
| v4, v5p | 2 | 1 (megacore) |
| v5e, v6e | 1 | 1 |
| Ironwood | 2 chiplets | 2 |
| 8t / 8i | 1 / 2 | not documented |

Ironwood's die-to-die link is 6× one ICI link: keep a chip's two chiplets on the busiest mesh axis.

</Slide>

<Slide title="Beyond the MXU">

- **SparseCore** (v4 onward): gather/scatter engines for embeddings, about 5% of die, 5–7× on embedding-heavy models. Pallas-programmable.
- **CAE** (8i): Collectives Acceleration Engine, up to 5× lower on-chip collective latency. The JAX table still lists a SparseCore on 8i.
- **Trainium2**: 8 NeuronCores, each a 128×128 tensor engine plus vector, scalar and GPSIMD engines, SBUF + PSUM.

</Slide>

<Slide title="Where the GPU mapping breaks">

- A TensorCore is not a big SM: one instruction stream, no thread switching.
- A VPU lane is not a warp: contiguous tiles and masking, no per-thread addressing.
- Registers are inverted: about 32 MB on an H100 against about 256 KB per TPU core. A TPU spills to VMEM instead.

</Slide>
