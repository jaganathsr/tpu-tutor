<Slide title="Switch against torus">

<Cols>
<div>

**Switch (NVLink, NeuronSwitch)**

- One hop to every peer in 8, 72 or 144.
- In-switch reduction (SHARP).
- Cheap all-to-all and small collectives.
- Beyond the domain: scale-out at about 50 GB/s per GPU (H100).

</div>
<div>

**Torus (ICI)**

- 6 neighbours, multiple hops.
- No in-network reduction.
- Per-chip bandwidth constant to about 9,000 chips.
- Bisection grows as $N^{2/3}$.

</div>
</Cols>

</Slide>

| Workload | Advantage |
|---|---|

| MoE training | NVL72 inside 72; torus at multi-rack scale |
| Low-latency decode | NVL72 one hop; TPU 8i at most 7 hops |
| Long-context decode | most HBM bandwidth and capacity per dollar |
| Recommendation (embeddings) | TPU SparseCore |
| Changing ops, dynamic shapes | GPU |

<Slide title="Converging, not the same">

- GPU Tensor Cores grow (warp → SM → 2 SMs) and gain Tensor Memory: toward the MXU and VMEM.
- Trainium moved from torus to switch, and toward NVLink Fusion.
- TPU split: 8t torus for training, 8i Boardfly and 384 MB SRAM for inference.
- PyTorch moved to native backends on TPU and Trainium.
- Unchanged: 100+ SMs and eager execution against 2 cores and whole-program compilation.

</Slide>
